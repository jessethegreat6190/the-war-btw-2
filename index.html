01 The Opening

A visit, a denial, and the information battle that followed

On 25 January 2025, Denise Nyakeru Tshisekedi, First Lady of the Democratic Republic of Congo, walked the wards of Camp Kokolo and Tshatshi Military Hospital in Kinshasa. The men in the beds had been wounded during the M23 offensive that had occurred the previous week, in which rebel forces captured Goma, the largest city in eastern Congo.

Her visit was documented and circulated by Congolese state media: a nation’s first lady at the bedside of its soldiers. On X, a different version of that same visit was also in circulation. Across dozens of accounts, a coordinated wave of posts claimed that the men Denise Nyakeru had visited were not Congolese soldiers at all, but fighters from the FDLR.

“The operation was designed not for mass persuasion, but to place one argument inside a specific institutional conversation that the DRC government, not Rwanda, was the aggressor.”

That wave of posts was not addressed to Congolese citizens. It was addressed, in English, to @UN and @EUCouncil: the specific institutions with the power to sanction, to condemn, to legitimise or delegitimise. When sanctions follow a conflict, they create a named class of losers with strong incentives to fight back.

Key timeline

1994 Genocide against the Tutsi in Rwanda. FDLR forms in eastern Congo.
1996–2003 Two Congo wars. Rwanda and Uganda intervene. Millions dead.
2012 M23 first emerges, captures Goma, then goes dormant.
2021 M23 re-emerges, begins advancing in North Kivu.
Jan 2025 Goma falls. The information war intensifies.
Mar 2025 EU sanctions. The covert network’s final wave.
Jun 2025 Washington Accords signed. Fighting continues as of mid-2026.

Three actors, three positions

DRC

Foreign aggression by Rwanda. Sovereign state entitled to international protection.

Rwanda

Defensive action. The international community failed to neutralise the FDLR for 30 years.

M23/AFC

Indigenous Congolese movement with legitimate grievances, not a Rwandan proxy.

03 Three Operations

Three operations. One information space.

The investigation identified three distinct campaigns operating simultaneously, each with a different author, target, and theory of how narratives win wars.

Operation 01

The Anti-Tshisekedi Burst Network

This campaign’s stated target, in 181 of 612 posts, was @UN and @EUCouncil, tagged as opening tokens. The operation was designed not for mass persuasion, but to place one argument inside a specific institutional conversation: that the DRC government, not Rwanda, was the aggressor.

90accounts
612posts
181tagged @UN/EU
71Ktotal views

N1: Tshisekedi as FDLR patron

The DRC president is recast from the leader of an invaded state into the financier of a genocidal militia.

N2: The DRC as genocidal aggressor

FARDC airstrikes on M23-aligned positions are reframed as the aerial extermination of Banyamulenge and Tutsi civilians.

N3: Tshisekedi as obstacle to peace

International sanctions framed as rewarding the victim narrative of an aggressor.

Feature: Burst posting

Multiple accounts published their entire quarterly output in windows of seconds to minutes. Several posts sharing the same timestamp to the second. The pattern is consistent with operators pasting from a prepared list.

  • MooniPeter: 10 posts in 11 sec
  • NKOTANYI1_: 21 posts in 30 sec
  • FrancoisOb1705: 20 posts in 2.6 min
  • TuyisengeD22748: 28 posts in 7.8 min

Feature: Script numbering

Two accounts published posts carrying the line numbers of the source document the text was copied from. @BertherNis26873 posted a list item numbered “1.” and @umuzunguwashomy posted “45.” implying a master list running to at least 45 entries.

Operation 02

The Anti-Kagame Hashtag Flooder

A single, dedicated operative achieved more reach than a 90-account network through surgical hashtag hijacking and curation. Its target was a search surface: the organic information environment anyone encounters when looking up the conflict.

1account
152posts
200median views/post
36Ktotal views

N1: The double genocide framing

The account’s bio claimed Kagame sent disguised troops to kill Tutsi families in Rwanda between 1990 and 1994, then presented himself as their saviour.

N2: Amplification of diaspora media

Rather than producing original content, the account curated: VOA Afrique, TV5 Monde, Radio Itahuka, Radio Inkingi, framing each link with its fixed hashtag block.

Feature: Fixed hashtag block

Every post carried an identical 13-hashtag block mixing neutral place names with attack hashtags. Appeared in 109 of 152 posts.

  • #rubavu #gisenyi #rwanda #burundi #kigali #kinshasa
  • #Africa #congo #goma #guerre
  • #KagameIsKilling #KagameIsACriminal #RDFNefativeForce

Feature: Engagement paradox

One account outperforms a 90-account network. @RwandaN41033494 achieved a median of 200 views per post. The entire 90-account covert network achieved a median of 14.

Operation 03

CongolaisTelema

The DRC government’s domestic mobilisation response to the M23 offensive. Fronted by Communications Minister Patrick Muyaya and carried on national broadcaster RTNC, it operated openly at scale with verified accounts and institutional reach.

215accounts
336posts
1.5Mpeak week views
245peak day posts

N1: National unity and FARDC recruitment

The campaign’s primary register was patriotic mobilisation: the army needs soldiers, the country needs solidarity.

N2: Cultural resistance

A “Front Culturel” of musicians and slam poets gave the campaign a civilian face beyond official government communications.

Feature: Verbatim duplication

State text appeared word for word across nominally independent journalist and influencer accounts. Identical texts by @TosuwaPaul and @chango_wa.

Feature: Hashtag infiltration

The covert network placed 190 posts carrying #CongolaisTelema alongside #TshisekediIsKilling, inserting genocide accusations into the search results of the DRC government’s own hashtag.

04 What It Means

The target was never the crowd

Propaganda, in its classical form, is a mass exercise. Saturate enough minds, shift the mood of a population, and you shift what governments feel they can do. The megaphone is its instrument. The crowd was its target.

“The prevailing instinct, when confronted with influence operations, is to reach for the label of misinformation: to find the lie and correct it. That instinct fails here, because there is no clean lie to correct.”

What this investigation documents is not simply a disinformation campaign. It is a campaign that understood, with some precision, that the battlefield for this conflict had shifted. Not to African television networks or newspaper front pages, but to a digital space with almost no equivalent rules, gatekeepers, or friction one where a numbered list, a few dozen disposable accounts, and knowledge of when the relevant institution meets is sufficient infrastructure for an operation aimed at the Security Council.

That shift matters because the old instincts for countering narrative manipulation were built for a different terrain. When the battleground was media, the intervention point was the editor, the fact-checker, the broadcaster's standards desk. In the environment this operation exploited, none of those friction points exist.

“What this investigation documented a network pushing the narrative that the FDLR was the real problem, that Rwanda's posture was defensive, that Tshisekedi was the obstacle to peace did not, on the evidence available, cause that outcome. But the frame those operations were working to install in New York and Brussels in March 2025 is recognisably present in the architecture of the agreement that followed.”

The UN documents FARDC cooperation with FDLR elements. Anti-Tutsi violence in the DRC is real. The FARDC did bomb Minembwe, and Banyamulenge civilians do live there. The network's work was not fabrication. It was re-voicing: taking documented facts, stripping their context, removing attribution, and reassembling them into a picture that served a particular conclusion.

This is what the FIMI framework was developed to capture and what the misinformation framing misses. The problem is not always false information. It is true information, selectively assembled and re-voiced through personas designed to appear as the spontaneous testimony of civilians.

“That gap is the operational environment this network exploited. The asymmetry between the sophistication of the manipulation and the capacity of the defence is not incidental to this story. It is the story.”
